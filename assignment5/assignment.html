Observations

People
To decice what to make, some of the individuals involved shared their ideas. The other group members would discuss the idea until concencus around a 'fun' idea was reached.
Once the group agreed, it became quickly apparant that for the sake of efficiency the main body and peripherals should be built in parallel by one or two builders.
A consequence of this, the connection between the body and peripherals was not planned.
Objects
The makedo system required more effort than the splitpins. Thus, makedo was ignored. This left the group with cardboard, hot glue and splitpins that was used extensivelt
The biggest challenge would end up being the way to connect the different parts to the body. Given the requirement that the servo motors had to be removable, hot glue was no option. Hence, there was a high reliance on splitpins for connection points and cardboard as building frame
Environment
Given the table taking up space between group members and the relatively loud environment most of the collaboration happened only with direct neighbours as opposed to the person across the table
The scarcity of the hot glue guns in the environment made it such that using them would require you to wait for others to finish using them, resulting in little glue experimentation Using it when deemed a requirement, not to test and iterate.

Messages
The smaller groups would discuss the technical aspects of how to build up the part they were working on. Between groups there were logistical discussions on how to combine parts
The ideas were externalized by manner of gestures. Holding roughly shaped pieces of cardboard together next to one another, mimicking the movement the motors would (hopefully) reproduce.
One of the few, maybe the only, misunderstanding was the exact way to attach the wings. This ended up getting quiclky corrected using gestures while holding roughly wing shaped cardboard bits

Services
The initial prompt caused the group to go into the direction of what we will call 'function over form'. The time constraint and cheap materials caused the builders to hack something together without measuring much.

For most of the constructing, very little scaffolding was required.
For the movement of the mechanism that was to be recorded, scaffolding was needed to get a grasp of how to use the control panel.
