Tinkering materials

material Self guiding Preciousness Threshold Ceiling Interoperable Seed power Portability Feedback
BYOR It does well enough. All parts use the same connector wires and the arrows on the motherboard indicate input/output Pretty high, having the circuitboards exposed makes them feel delicate A low threshold, even if you are not familiar with electronics you can plug in the connections and just see what happens The ceiling is quite low with the limited sensors, outputs and inability to connect multiple outputs on one input There is no built in interoperability. But, the BYOR does seem to make more sense when combining it with a frame it can manipulate The quick and simple building of the electronic side, practically begs for also cobbling something together for the electronic parts to act on While the main controller of the BYOR is rather small, once an actual robot has been built it can be quite bulky Very high. Half the components of the BYOR move, make noise or make light. The sensors do not give immediate feedback, but are easy to link to the world space affecting components
Lego Very good. The studs on lego immediately make it clear they can lock into something. That something conveniently happens to be the bottom of another brick Quite low. Lego is just plastic after all, there's very little intrensic value. Unless you happen to have been the one paying for the pieces with lego prices these days A very low threshold, the biggest thing holding you back is creativity The ceiling is high. Especially when adding in more 'advanced' lego, scaling from lego technic to lego mindstorms There is no built in interoperability. But, you can get creative with glue Average. Picking up a new 'weirdly' shaped lego brick can help you think of some new ways to incorporate it. But at some point, your own creativity is more of a seed than the bricks Given that most of lego is hollow and quite firmly connected, it is easy to transport There is the audible and tactile feedback of clicking a block in place. But there is little feedback of what the final build will be
Little bits Neutral. The magnets are great and make connecting parts together. However, my first instinct was to connect similarly colored components, which does not always translate into a functional sequence Around neutral. It is unlikely you will break the parts on purposes to use them, but the free wires feel a bit delicate The threshold is on the lower end. You can click together the parts as indicated on the example cards The ceiling is very high. On one hand, you have all the parts, knobs and options to manipulate. But with the arduino block, you add on a lot of the capabilities of arduino There is interoperability with arduino High. There are so many different parts that one of them is bound to catch your interest The components are held together just with magnets and can easily come apart. While little bits does offer mounting boards, these are not part of their starter kit. A lot of little bits consits of control modules. While these will give audible feedback once hooked up to the speaker, that does require an extra step
Arduino kit next to non existant. You need to know about electrical circuits to connect even a basic part Quite high. Similar to BYOR, the exposed motherboard comes across as delicate Very high. Even if all you want to do is make the onboard led blink, you need to be familiar with C-based object oriented programming The ceiling is very high. You can hook up just about any electrical component to an arduino as long as you can supply the required logic Low to neutral. If you can solder, you can work arduino into most electrical circuits. But your ability to solder is not inherent to arduino. On the other hand, arduino does have mounting holes for attaching to a frame High. With all manner of sensors and output possibilities you have near endless distinct options While the arduino boards themselves are easy to move around, once they are attached to components the wiring becomes a weak point This is quite similar to BYOR, but slightly worse. To be able to connect the different components, the extra step of programming them needs to be taken
Snap circuits While there is a grid that all the components can snap into easily, making for nice self guidance, it is not immediately clear what components should be connected together The components have a low preciousness. While a tinkerer may not want to permanently alter them, they are not incredibly precious Since the components can be snapped together and are kept in a 2d array, the threshold it quite low. There is a high ceiling. While consisting of simple components, building a functioning radio is done often Non existant. Due to the proprietary connectors, it becomes difficult to find a way to add a different material High. With all the different components, this kit is like an electrical engineering bachelor in a box The starter kit offers a mounting board. Given the rigid connections, this make it incredibly easy to move finished projects There exist components that give the tinkerer feedback, like BYOR. However, the connections between components require more effort
Geomag Due to the magnetic nature of the components, putting them together happens almost by default Very low. The pieces are just shiny steel and plastic with metal imbedded Due to the almost self connecting nature of the magnetic components, the threshold is incredibly low The ceiling is on the lower end when considering just the pieces given by basic kit. However the ceiling can be quite high when including other magnetic components Given that the principle of this kit is magnetism, any sort of ferromagnetic item can be used to connect the pieces to as well Low. While the kit can be a great addition to other projects, it lacks a factor that surprise the user If the frame or shape created is stable, it becomes easy to transport. However the steel balls allow for the magnets to slide if they are not somehow fixed Similar to the lego blocks, there is the feedback received when connecting two blocks together. But there is no feedback related to when the project is finished

Wide walled projects

Weather station
Arduino can be used for a whole bunch of projects based on
the different modules that can be connected the main board.
An example of this is shown in this weather station project.
Turning an arduino microcontroller into a weather station
is a wide wall aproach of the tinkering kit.
From a number of all purpose sensors, a few are selected
and connected such that the micro controller can function as a small weather station.
weather station project
Lego league
The first lego league uses lego to set up problems
on a table where kids can make a robot to solve the issues.
The robot is made using lego mindstorms.

This is a wide walled lego project, given that the team behind
the first lego league is using lego not just to build something.
But to use it to represent societal issues in a manner that encourages young students to interact with and possibly solve them. first lego league
A KNEX claw
This project uses the KNEX building pieces to create a motorized claw
This shows how wide walled KNEX is,
because the standard KNEX building pieces are used
to create a functioning claw which is not one of the standard designs. KNEX claw
