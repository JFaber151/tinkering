Tinkering materials

material Self guiding Preciousness Threshold Ceiling Interoperable Seed power
BYOR It does well enough. All parts use the same connector wires and the arrows on the motherboard indicate input/output Pretty high, having the circuitboards exposed makes them feel delicate A low threshold, even if you are not familiar with electronics you can plug in the connections and just see what happens The ceiling is quite low with the limited sensors, outputs and inability to connect multiple outputs on one input There is no built in interoperability. But, the BYOR does seem to make more sense when combining it with a frame it can manipulate The quick and simple building of the electronic side, practically begs for also cobbling something together for the electronic parts to act on
Lego Very good. The studs on lego immediately make it clear they can lock into something. That something conveniently happens to be the bottom of another brick Quite low. Lego is just plastic after all, there's very little intrensic value. Unless you happen to have been the one paying for the pieces with lego prices these days A very low threshold, the biggest thing holding you back is creativity The ceiling is high. Especially when adding in more 'advanced' lego scaling from lego technic to lego mindstorms There is no built in interoperability. But, you can get creative with glue Average. Picking up a new 'weirdly' shaped lego brick can help you think of some new ways to incorporate it. But at some point, your own creativity is more of a seed than the bricks
Little bits Neutral. The magnets are great and make connecting parts together. However, my first instinct was to connect similarly colored components, which does not always translate into a functional sequence Around neutral. It is unlikely you will break the parts on purposes to use them, but the free wires feel a bit delicate The threshold is on the lower end. You can click together the parts as indicated on the example cards The ceiling is very high. On one hand, you have all the parts, knobs and options to manipulate. But with the arduino block, you add on a lot of the capabilities of arduino There is interoperability with arduino High. There are so many different parts that one of them is bound to catch your interest
Arduino kit next to non existant. You need to know about electrical circuits to connect even a basic part Quite high. Similar to BYOR, the exposed motherboard comes across as delicate Very high. Even if all you want to do is make the onboard led blink, you need to be familiar with C-based object oriented programming The ceiling is very high. You can hook up just about any electrical component to an arduino as long as you can supply the required logic Low to neutral. If you can solder, you can work arduino into most electrical circuits. But your ability to solder is not inherent to arduino. On the other hand, arduino does have mounting holes for attaching to a frame High. With all manner of sensors and output possibilities you have near endless distinct options
